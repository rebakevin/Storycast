Read the summary

Written by William Shakespeare in the 1590s, Romeo and Juliet is set in fair Verona, where two noble families have fought for generations. Romeo of the Montagues and Juliet of the Capulets fall in love at a masked feast and marry in secret. A duel, a banishment and a letter that never arrives tear them apart, and they die for a love that came minutes too late. Only then do their families make peace.

The world of Verona

A city that cannot forget a quarrel.

Written by William Shakespeare in the 1590s, Romeo and Juliet is set in fair Verona, where two noble families have fought for generations. Their servants brawl in the streets; their children inherit the quarrel before they can understand it.

The Montagues

Charcoal line · Romeo

Romeo's house. Hot-blooded and loyal, they answer insult with steel.

The Capulets

Crimson line · Juliet

Juliet's house. Proud and wealthy, they guard their name above their daughter.

Meet the characters

  1. I

    Romeo Montague

    The lover

    A young, restless romantic, sick with love for a girl who does not love him — until he meets Juliet and forgets her entirely.

  2. II

    Juliet Capulet

    The daughter

    Not yet fourteen, sharp-minded and brave. She is the first to say what love will cost, and the first to pay it.

  3. III

  4. IV

    Juliet's fiery cousin. For him the feud is a point of honour, and Romeo's presence at the feast is an insult.

II · The Journey →

Chapter II · Four scenes

The Journey

Romeo in charcoal, Juliet in crimson. Watch how close they stand in each scene.

  1. i.

    The Capulet party

    Romeo slips into the Capulet feast in disguise, hoping only to glimpse Rosaline. Instead he meets Juliet. They speak in a shared sonnet, and kiss before either learns the other’s name.

  2. ii.

    The balcony & the secret marriage

    Beneath her window Romeo overhears Juliet wish away her name. They vow to love in spite of it. Next day Friar Laurence marries them in secret, hoping it will end the feud.

  3. iii.

    Mercutio & Tybalt fall

    Tybalt challenges Romeo, who refuses to fight his new kinsman. Mercutio fights instead and is killed. Blind with grief, Romeo kills Tybalt, and the feud claims a new debt.

  4. iv.

    Banishment & the desperate plan

    The Prince spares Romeo’s life but banishes him from Verona. With Juliet promised to another man, Friar Laurence offers a way out: a potion that will make her seem dead, and a letter to bring Romeo home.

A letter is sent. It does not arrive.

III · The Tragedy →

Chapter III · The last night

The Tragedy

Juliet's plan

Juliet drinks the Friar's potion and is laid in the Capulet tomb, cold as stone. But the messenger carrying Laurence's letter is stopped by plague quarantine. Romeo never reads it.

The final tragedy

Romeo hears only that Juliet is dead. He buys poison, breaks into the tomb, and drinks beside her. Moments later she wakes, finds him lifeless, and takes his dagger. Both die for a love that came minutes too late.

Reconciliation

At dawn, the Prince gathers both houses at the tomb. Montague and Capulet, finally face to face, see what their hatred has cost. They clasp hands and promise statues of gold for the children they lost.

What the story means

Love
It arrives at first sight and outruns every rule. Love gives Romeo and Juliet courage, and it gives them no time.
Fate
A plague, a missed letter, a minute’s delay. The play calls its lovers star-crossed, yet each small failure was human.
Hatred
An old grudge no one can explain. It is inherited, not chosen, and it poisons everyone it touches.
Consequences
Every choice, from a duel to a secret, adds weight. Peace comes only after the cost is counted in children.

Read the summary

Nearly 3,000 years old, the Odyssey follows Odysseus home from the Trojan War. A voyage of two weeks takes ten years: he blinds a Cyclops and shouts his name, drawing down the curse of the sea god Poseidon, and loses every ship and every man to monsters, storms and his crew's mistakes. Twenty years after he left, he reaches Ithaca disguised as a beggar, defeats the suitors crowding his palace, and proves who he is to his wife Penelope.

Chapter II · The Wrong Turns →

The world of the Odyssey

Ten years of sea between a king and his home.

After ten years of war, the Greeks take Troy with a wooden horse, and Odysseus sets out for Ithaca, where his wife Penelope and his son Telemachus wait. The sea, the gods and his own pride stand in the way. Nobody he sailed with makes it home.

  2. II

II · The Wrong Turns →

Chapter II · Five stops

The Wrong Turns

Every stop on the way home goes a little worse than the last.

  1. i.

    Ismarus & the Lotus Eaters

    Day one · A storm · Forgotten homes

    Odysseus raids the city of Ismarus, but his men stay on the beach to feast instead of leaving. The Cicones return and kill 72 of them. A storm then blows the fleet to the Lotus Eaters, where anyone who eats the lotus forgets their home and family.

  2. ii.

    The Cyclops

    Polyphemus's cave · “My name is Nobody”

    Odysseus and twelve men are trapped in a cave by a one-eyed giant who eats two of them each morning. Odysseus gets Polyphemus drunk, tells him his name is Nobody, and drives a burning stake into his eye. When the other Cyclopes ask who is hurting him, he answers, “Nobody.” The men escape strapped under the bellies of his sheep.

  3. iii.

    A name shouted

    The sea · Poseidon's curse

    Odysseus could simply have sailed away. Instead he shouts his real name at the blinded giant. Polyphemus prays to his father Poseidon that Odysseus never reach home, or arrive late, broken and alone. From that moment the sea itself is his enemy.

  4. iv.

    The bag of winds

    Aeolus · Ithaca in sight

    Aeolus, king of the winds, seals the dangerous winds in a leather bag and sends a gentle wind home. For nine days it works, and on the tenth Ithaca appears. Odysseus falls asleep, his crew open the bag, and the winds blow every ship back across the sea.

  5. v.

    The Laestrygonians

    A harbour of giants · Twelve ships become one

    Giants attack from the cliffs, hurling boulders and spearing sailors like fish. Eleven of the twelve ships are destroyed. Only Odysseus's ship, moored outside the harbour, escapes.

One ship is left. Then, a witch.

Chapter III · Two scenes

Circe & the Dead

A year of magic, and a journey to speak with the dead.

  1. i.

    Circe's island

    Aeaea · A feast · A year

    On the island of Aeaea, the witch goddess Circe feeds a scouting party and turns every one of them into a pig. Hermes gives Odysseus a plant called moly that blocks her magic. Her spell fails, she frees his men, and they stay on the island for a year while Penelope waits at home.

  2. ii.

    The land of the dead

    The edge of the world · A warning · His mother

    Circe tells him he must first speak to the prophet Tiresias. Odysseus sails to the edge of the world, digs a pit, fills it with blood and summons the dead. Tiresias warns him never to touch the cattle of the sun god Helios. Odysseus also sees his mother, who died of grief waiting for him, and three times she slips through his arms like a shadow.

Now, the most dangerous water in the ancient world.

IV · Deadly Waters →

Chapter IV · Four trials

Deadly Waters

Sirens, a monster, a broken vow, and seven years on a beach.

  1. i.

    The Sirens

    Beautiful voices · Beeswax and a mast

    The Sirens' voices lure sailors onto the rocks. Odysseus has his crew plug their ears with beeswax and tie him to the mast, so that he can hear the song without steering toward it.

  2. ii.

    Scylla & Charybdis

    A narrow strait · No safe path

    A six-headed monster waits on one side and a great whirlpool on the other. Odysseus chooses Scylla, knowing it will cost him six men rather than the whole ship.

  3. iii.

    The cattle of the sun

    Thrinacia · Starving · A thunderbolt

    Bad winds trap the ship on the island of the sun god's sacred cattle until the food runs out. While Odysseus sleeps, his crew slaughter the cattle. The moment they sail, Zeus splits the ship with a thunderbolt. Every man drowns except Odysseus, who clings to the wreckage.

  4. iv.

    Calypso & Phaeacia

    Seven years · A raft · A ship home

    The sea washes Odysseus onto the island of the goddess Calypso. She offers him immortality, but he sits on the beach for seven years, crying and staring at the horizon. At last Athena persuades Zeus to intervene. Odysseus builds a raft, Poseidon smashes it, and the king of Phaeacia gives him a ship. He wakes on the beach of Ithaca twenty years after he left.

Twenty years on, he wakes on the shore of Ithaca.

V · The Return →

Chapter V · Four scenes

The Return

Home at last, but not yet recognised.

  1. i.

    A palace full of suitors

    Ithaca · 108 men · A shroud

    Over a hundred men have moved into Odysseus's palace, eating his food and pressing Penelope to marry one of them. She has stalled for years by promising to choose once she finishes a burial shroud, weaving by day and unravelling by night. Athena disguises Odysseus as an old beggar so he can walk in unrecognised.

  2. ii.

    Argos

    The gate · A dog that waited

    At the gate, an ancient dog lying on a pile of manure lifts his head. It is Argos, the hunting dog Odysseus raised as a puppy. The dog wags his tail, lays his ears flat and dies. He had waited twenty years to see his master once more.

  3. iii.

    The bow

    The great hall · Twelve axes

    The suitors mock the beggar and throw food at him. Penelope sets a contest: whoever strings Odysseus's great bow and shoots an arrow through twelve axe heads wins her hand. No suitor can even bend it. The beggar strings it, fires cleanly through all twelve, and turns the bow on the suitors. With Telemachus beside him, he kills them all.

  4. iv.

    The bed

    Penelope's last test

    Twenty years is a long time, and Penelope is not yet convinced. She tells her servants to move the king's bed out of the bedroom. Odysseus answers that it cannot be moved: he built it around a living olive tree, and one of its posts is the tree itself. Only the real Odysseus could know that. Penelope breaks down, and they are reunited.

What the story means

Pride
The smartest man in the room still cannot control his ego. One shouted name turns a two-week voyage into ten years.
Cunning
A wooden horse, a name that is Nobody, a ride under a sheep. Odysseus wins by thinking his way out.
Loyalty
A wife who unravels her weaving, a dog who waits twenty years, a son who stands beside his father.
Consequences
Every ship and every man lost on the way traces back to a choice, from the feast on the beach to the cattle of the sun.

The voyage is over. Choose another.

Read the summary

In the summer of 1922, Nick Carraway moves to Long Island and finds himself next door to the mysterious Jay Gatsby. Gatsby throws enormous parties in the hope that Daisy Buchanan, the woman he loved five years ago, will walk in. Nick brings them together again, but Daisy's husband Tom, a fatal accident and Gatsby's own past close in. By the end of the summer Gatsby is dead, and Nick is left to look across the bay at the green light.

The dream
Gatsby's relentless pursuit of a bright future is beautiful, and it crumbles into delusion.
The past
Gatsby wants to repeat what happened five years ago. It is over, and the current keeps pulling him back.
Class
Old money in East Egg looks down on new money in West Egg, and treats Gatsby rudely even as it enjoys his parties.
Carelessness
The Buchanans leave others to pay for their mistakes. Daisy does not own up to the accident, and Tom sends Wilson to Gatsby's door.
